Introduction

Where We Are

  • Lecture 6: ARMA estimation by (quasi-)MLE, with a sandwich variance
  • Applied work often regresses \(y_t\) on \(x_t\), \(x_{t-1}\), \(y_{t-1}\), etc. by OLS
  • OLS asks for less than MLE: moment conditions, no full distribution
  • To do inference, we need asymptotic theory for dependent data
  • Lindeberg–Lévy and Lindeberg–Feller need independent observations
  • Time series are dependent by design: that was the whole point of ARMA models!
  • So the textbook LLN and CLT do not apply. Where exactly do their proofs break?

Today

  • Does \(\bar y_T\) still converge to \(\mu\)?
  • What is the asymptotic variance of \(\sqrt{T}(\bar y_T - \mu)\)?
  • How do we get standard errors that are robust to autocorrelation?
  • Spoiler: yes; it is the long-run variance, not \(\gamma_0\); Newey-West
  • We will sketch some proofs and give references for the rest
  • Focus on the main ideas, not the technical details

The Law of Large Numbers

The Variance of the Sample Mean

  • \(\{y_t\}\) is covariance-stationary with mean \(\mu\) and autocovariances \(\gamma_h\)
  • As usual, assume absolute summability: \(\sum_{h=-\infty}^{\infty} |\gamma_h| < \infty\)
  • The sample mean \(\bar y_T = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T y_t\) is unbiased: \(\E[\bar y_T] = \mu\)
  • Stack \(\symbf{Y}_T = (y_1 - \mu, \ldots, y_T - \mu)'\) and let \(\symbf{1}_T\) be a \(T \times 1\) vector of ones
  • Then \(\bar y_T - \mu = \frac{1}{T}\symbf{1}_T'\symbf{Y}_T\). With \(\symbf{V}_T \equiv \Var(\symbf{Y}_T)\):

\[ \E\left(\bar y_T - \mu\right)^2 = \frac{1}{T^2}\,\symbf{1}_T'\symbf{V}_T\symbf{1}_T = \frac{\text{sum of all entries of } \symbf{V}_T}{T^2} \]

  • Sanity check: i.i.d. data give \(\symbf{V}_T = \gamma_0\symbf{I}_T\), hence \(\gamma_0/T\)

Summing the Covariance Matrix

\[ \symbf{V}_T = \begin{pmatrix} \gamma_0 & \gamma_1 & \gamma_2 & \cdots & \gamma_{T-1} \\ \gamma_1 & \gamma_0 & \gamma_1 & \cdots & \gamma_{T-2} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \gamma_{T-1} & \gamma_{T-2} & \gamma_{T-3} & \cdots & \gamma_0 \end{pmatrix} \]

  • Lag \(h\) fills the \(h\)-th diagonals above and below the main one: \(T - |h|\) entries each

\[ \sum_{i=1}^T\sum_{j=1}^T \gamma_{|i-j|} = T\gamma_0 + 2(T-1)\gamma_1 + \cdots + 2\gamma_{T-1} = \sum_{h=-(T-1)}^{T-1}(T - |h|)\,\gamma_h \]

\[ \implies \boxed{\E\left(\bar y_T - \mu\right)^2 = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{h=-(T-1)}^{T-1}\left(1 - \frac{|h|}{T}\right)\gamma_h} \]

A Weak Law of Large Numbers

  • Each weight \(1 - |h|/T\) lies in \([0, 1]\), so:

\[ \E\left(\bar y_T - \mu\right)^2 \le \frac{1}{T}\sum_{h=-(T-1)}^{T-1}|\gamma_h| \le \frac{1}{T}\underbrace{\sum_{h=-\infty}^{\infty}|\gamma_h|}_{< \infty} \longrightarrow 0 \]

  • \(\bar y_T \to \mu\) in mean square, hence \(\bar y_T \xrightarrow{p} \mu\) by Chebyshev’s inequality
  • This is a weak LLN for covariance-stationary time series
  • No independence needed: only covariance stationarity and absolute summability
  • Absolute summability: “the process can be dependent, but not too dependent”

The Long-Run Variance

  • \(\Var(\bar y_T)\) shrinks like \(1/T\). Does \(T \cdot \Var(\bar y_T)\) settle down?

Proposition (Hamilton, Prop. 7.5)

If \(\{y_t\}\) is covariance-stationary with absolutely summable autocovariances, then \[ \lim_{T\to\infty} T\cdot\E\left(\bar y_T - \mu\right)^2 = \sum_{h=-\infty}^{\infty}\gamma_h \equiv \omega^2 \]

  • \(\omega^2\) is the long-run variance of \(\{y_t\}\)
  • Proof: each weight \(1 - |h|/T \to 1\), and \(|\gamma_h|\) dominates. An \(\varepsilon\)-proof is in the appendix
  • AR(1): \(\omega^2 = \frac{\sigma^2}{(1-\phi)^2}\) versus \(\gamma_0 = \frac{\sigma^2}{1-\phi^2}\), a ratio of \(\frac{1+\phi}{1-\phi}\). With \(\phi = 0.9\): \(19\)!

The Vector Case

  • Same algebra for a mean-zero, covariance-stationary vector \(\symbf{g}_t \in \R^k\)
  • Autocovariance matrices: \(\symbf{\Gamma}_h \equiv \E\left[\symbf{g}_t\symbf{g}_{t-h}'\right]\). Notice that \(\symbf{\Gamma}_{-h} = \symbf{\Gamma}_h'\)
  • The long-run variance becomes a matrix:

\[ \symbf{\Omega} \equiv \lim_{T\to\infty} T\cdot\Var\left(\bar{\symbf{g}}_T\right) = \sum_{h=-\infty}^{\infty}\symbf{\Gamma}_h = \symbf{\Gamma}_0 + \sum_{h=1}^{\infty}\left(\symbf{\Gamma}_h + \symbf{\Gamma}_h'\right) \]

  • Keep an eye on \(\symbf{\Omega}\): it shows up in every limit distribution today
  • OLS: \(\symbf{g}_t = \symbf{x}_t u_t\), the regression scores
  • GMM, later in the course: \(\symbf{g}_t\) is the vector of moment functions

Central Limit Theorems

Martingale Difference Sequences

  • Independence is one thing. Dependence comes in all shapes and forms!
  • So there is no such thing as “the CLT for time series”

Definition (Martingale Difference Sequence)

\(\{y_t\}\) is an MDS with respect to the information sets \(\{\mathcal{F}_t\}\) if \(y_t\) is known given \(\mathcal{F}_t\), \(\E|y_t| < \infty\), and \(\E[y_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = 0\) almost surely.

  • An MDS has mean zero and \(\gamma_h = 0\) for \(h \neq 0\): uncorrelated, yet possibly dependent
  • Example: \(y_t = e_t e_{t-1}\) with \(e_t\) i.i.d., mean zero, variance \(\sigma^2\), and \(\mathcal{F}_t = \sigma(e_s : s \le t)\)
  • MDS: \(\E[y_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = e_{t-1}\E[e_t] = 0\)
  • Dependent: \(\Cov(y_t^2, y_{t-1}^2) = \sigma^4\left(\E[e_t^4] - \sigma^4\right) = \sigma^4\Var(e_t^2) > 0\) unless \(e_t^2\) is constant

A CLT for MDS

Theorem (MDS CLT, Hamilton, Prop. 7.8)

Let \(\{y_t\}\) be a scalar MDS with \(\E[y_t^2] = \sigma_t^2 > 0\) such that:

  • \(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\sigma_t^2 \to \sigma^2 > 0\)
  • \(\E|y_t|^r < \infty\) for some \(r > 2\) and all \(t\)
  • \(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T y_t^2 \xrightarrow{p} \sigma^2\)

Then \(\sqrt{T}\,\bar y_T = \frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_{t=1}^T y_t \xrightarrow{d} N(0, \sigma^2)\).

  • No autocovariances to add up: the long-run variance is just \(\sigma^2\)
  • Strictly stationary and ergodic MDS: finite variance suffices (Hansen, Thm. 14.11)
  • Extends to vectors (Cramér–Wold) and to triangular arrays

Strong Mixing

  • Without the MDS structure, we must control how fast dependence fades
  • For two events \(A\) and \(B\): \(\alpha(A, B) \equiv \left|\Pr(A \cap B) - \Pr(A)\Pr(B)\right|\), zero under independence
  • \(\mathcal{F}_{-\infty}^{t} = \sigma(\ldots, y_{t-1}, y_t)\) collects the past, and \(\mathcal{F}_{t}^{\infty} = \sigma(y_t, y_{t+1}, \ldots)\) the future

\[ \alpha(\ell) \equiv \sup_t\ \sup_{A \in \mathcal{F}_{-\infty}^{t-\ell},\ B \in \mathcal{F}_{t}^{\infty}} \alpha(A, B) \]

  • The process is strong mixing if \(\alpha(\ell) \to 0\) as \(\ell \to \infty\)
  • Far-apart events become nearly independent. Faster decay means weaker dependence
  • i.i.d. data: \(\alpha(\ell) = 0\) for \(\ell \ge 1\)
  • Stationary ARMA with well-behaved shocks are strong mixing (Hansen, Thm. 14.26)

A CLT for Mixing Processes

Theorem (Hansen, Thm. 14.15)

Let \(\symbf{g}_t\) be strictly stationary, mean zero, and strong mixing, with, for some \(r > 2\): \[ \E\lVert\symbf{g}_t\rVert^r < \infty \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\alpha(\ell)^{1 - 2/r} < \infty \] Then \(\symbf{\Omega} = \sum_{h}\symbf{\Gamma}_h\) converges and \(\sqrt{T}\,\bar{\symbf{g}}_T \xrightarrow{d} N(0, \symbf{\Omega})\).

  • Trade-off: more finite moments (larger \(r\)) allow slower mixing
  • Linear processes \(y_t = \mu + \sum_j\psi_j e_{t-j}\), \(e_t\) i.i.d., \(\sum_j|\psi_j| < \infty\): no mixing needed
  • For them, \(\sqrt{T}(\bar y_T - \mu) \xrightarrow{d} N(0, \omega^2)\), with \(\omega^2 = \sigma^2\left(\sum_j\psi_j\right)^2\) (Hamilton, Prop. 7.11)
  • The message is always the same: the asymptotic variance is the long-run variance

OLS with Time Series

Setup and Consistency

  • Linear model \(y_t = \symbf{x}_t'\symbf{\beta} + u_t\) with \(\E[\symbf{x}_t u_t] = 0\)
  • \(\symbf{x}_t\) is \(K \times 1\), includes the intercept, and may contain lags of \(y_t\)
  • \((y_t, \symbf{x}_t)\) is strictly stationary and ergodic, and \(\symbf{Q} \equiv \E[\symbf{x}_t\symbf{x}_t'] > 0\)
  • Then \(\symbf{\beta} = \symbf{Q}^{-1}\E[\symbf{x}_t y_t]\) is identified

\[ \hat{\symbf{\beta}}_T - \symbf{\beta} = \left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\symbf{x}_t\symbf{x}_t'\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\symbf{x}_t u_t\right) \]

  • Ergodic theorem (Lecture 3): both averages converge, so \(\hat{\symbf{\beta}}_T \xrightarrow{p} \symbf{\beta}\)
  • Inference hinges on \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\sum_t\symbf{x}_t u_t\): are the scores \(\symbf{g}_t = \symbf{x}_t u_t\) serially correlated?

Unforecastable Errors

  • Assume \(\E[u_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = 0\), where \(\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\) contains \(\symbf{x}_t\) and past errors
  • Example: an AR(2), with \(\symbf{x}_t = (1, y_{t-1}, y_{t-2})'\)
  • Then \(\E[\symbf{x}_t u_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = \symbf{x}_t\,\E[u_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = 0\): the scores are an MDS
  • MDS CLT plus finite fourth moments of \(y_t\) and \(\symbf{x}_t\) (Hansen, Thm. 14.35):

\[ \boxed{\sqrt{T}\left(\hat{\symbf{\beta}}_T - \symbf{\beta}\right) \xrightarrow{d} N\left(0, \symbf{Q}^{-1}\symbf{\Sigma}\symbf{Q}^{-1}\right), \qquad \symbf{\Sigma} = \E\left[\symbf{x}_t\symbf{x}_t' u_t^2\right]} \]

  • The same sandwich as in the heteroskedastic cross section

When Are the Scores Serially Correlated?

Too few lags. Inflation \(\pi_t\) follows an AR(2), but we fit an AR(1):

\[ \pi_t = \alpha + \rho\,\pi_{t-1} + u_t \]

  • The error absorbs the omitted lag: \(u_t\) still depends on \(\pi_{t-2}\), which is known at \(t-1\)
  • So \(\E[u_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \neq 0\), and the scores are not an MDS

Overlapping horizons. Monthly data, 12-month stock returns on the dividend yield \(d_t\):

\[ r_{t+1} + r_{t+2} + \cdots + r_{t+12} = \alpha + \beta\,d_t + u_{t+12} \]

  • Errors one month apart share 11 of their 12 monthly returns
  • Even if monthly returns are unforecastable, \(u_t\) is an MA(11): \(\symbf{\Gamma}_h = 0\) only for \(h \ge 12\)

Persistent Errors

Static regression. Aggregate consumption \(c_t\) on income \(w_t\):

\[ c_t = \alpha + \beta\,w_t + u_t \]

  • \(u_t\) collects slow-moving forces (wealth, habits, demographics), so it is persistent
  • Income \(w_t\) is persistent too
  • If \(w_t\) and \(u_t\) are independent with mean zero, the slope’s score \(w_t u_t\) has

\[ \E\left[w_t u_t \cdot w_{t-h}u_{t-h}\right] = \E\left[w_t w_{t-h}\right]\E\left[u_t u_{t-h}\right] = \gamma_w(h)\,\gamma_u(h) \]

  • Two AR(1)s with coefficient 0.9: \(w_t u_t\) has first autocorrelation \(0.9 \times 0.9 = 0.81\)

Serially Correlated Scores

  • In all three cases, \(\symbf{x}_t u_t\) is serially correlated, so the MDS CLT does not apply
  • Consistency survives: \(\hat{\symbf{\beta}}_T\) still converges to the projection coefficient
  • What changes is the asymptotic variance, hence inference
  • If \(y_t\) and \(\symbf{x}_t\) have finite \(r\)-th moments and \(\sum_{\ell}\alpha(\ell)^{1 - 4/r} < \infty\), for some \(r > 4\):

\[ \boxed{\sqrt{T}\left(\hat{\symbf{\beta}}_T - \symbf{\beta}\right) \xrightarrow{d} N\left(0, \symbf{Q}^{-1}\symbf{\Omega}\symbf{Q}^{-1}\right), \qquad \symbf{\Omega} = \sum_{h=-\infty}^{\infty}\E\left[\symbf{x}_t\symbf{x}_{t-h}' u_t u_{t-h}\right]} \]

  • \(\symbf{\Omega}\) is the long-run variance of \(\symbf{g}_t = \symbf{x}_t u_t\) (Hansen, Thm. 14.35)

HAC Estimation

The Easy Case: MDS Scores

  • \(\hat{\symbf{Q}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_t\symbf{x}_t\symbf{x}_t'\) is the easy part. The work is in the “meat”
  • With MDS scores, use the sample analogue of \(\symbf{\Sigma}\):

\[ \hat{\symbf{\Sigma}} = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\symbf{x}_t\symbf{x}_t'\hat u_t^2, \qquad \hat u_t = y_t - \symbf{x}_t'\hat{\symbf{\beta}}_T \]

  • This is White (1980)’s estimator, exactly as in the cross section
  • Robust to heteroskedasticity, not to autocorrelation
  • Standard errors: square roots of the diagonal of \(\hat{\symbf{Q}}^{-1}\hat{\symbf{\Sigma}}\hat{\symbf{Q}}^{-1}/T\)
  • Usual \(t\)-tests and Wald tests are valid

Estimating the Long-Run Variance

  • Write \(\hat{\symbf{g}}_t = \symbf{x}_t\hat u_t\) and estimate each autocovariance matrix:

\[ \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=h+1}^T\hat{\symbf{g}}_t\hat{\symbf{g}}_{t-h}', \qquad \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_{-h} = \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h' \]

  • Infinitely many \(\symbf{\Gamma}_h\), but they must fade as \(h\) grows. Far lags are estimated from few terms
  • Idea: pick a truncation lag \(q\) and drop the rest: \(\hat{\symbf{\Omega}}_q = \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_0 + \sum_{h=1}^{q}\left(\hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h + \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h'\right)\)
  • Fine if \(\symbf{\Gamma}_h = 0\) beyond lag \(q\), e.g. overlapping forecasts (Hansen and Hodrick (1980))
  • Two problems: \(\hat{\symbf{\Omega}}_q\) jumps around as \(q\) changes, and it need not be positive semi-definite
  • In the scalar case, it can even be negative: a negative variance! 🙃

A Negative Variance

  • Scalar case with \(q = 1\). Since \(\hat\gamma_1 = \hat\rho_1\hat\gamma_0\):

\[ \hat\Omega_1 = \hat\gamma_0 + 2\hat\gamma_1 = \hat\gamma_0\left(1 + 2\hat\rho_1\right) < 0 \iff \hat\rho_1 < -\frac{1}{2} \]

  • Example: difference a white noise \(e_t\) by mistake (“over-differencing”)

\[ y_t = e_t - e_{t-1}: \qquad \gamma_0 = 2\sigma^2, \quad \gamma_1 = -\sigma^2, \quad \gamma_h = 0 \text{ for } h \ge 2, \quad \rho_1 = -\frac{1}{2} \]

  • Truncating at \(q = 1\) is exact here: \(\omega^2 = \gamma_0 + 2\gamma_1 = 0\)

The Newey-West Estimator

  • Newey and West (1987): downweight far lags with the Bartlett kernel

\[ \hat{\symbf{\Omega}}_{NW} = \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_0 + \sum_{h=1}^{q}w_h\left(\hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h + \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h'\right), \qquad w_h = 1 - \frac{h}{q+1} \]

  • Positive semi-definite for every sample and every \(q\) (proof in the appendix)
  • Smooth in \(q\): no jumps as lags enter
  • Called a HAC estimator: heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent
  • Standard errors: square roots of the diagonal of \(\hat{\symbf{Q}}^{-1}\hat{\symbf{\Omega}}_{NW}\hat{\symbf{Q}}^{-1}/T\)
  • Consistency needs \(q \to \infty\), but not too fast
  • Under mixing, \(q^3/T = O(1)\) is sufficient (Hansen, Thm. 14.34)

  return ((x ^ (x >>> 16)) >>> 0) / 4294967296
}
nwShocks = {
  const draw = d3.randomNormal.source(d3.randomLcg(nwSeed(4111 + nw.seed)))(0, 1)
  return Float64Array.from({length: nwMaxT + 1}, draw)
}
nwOmega2 = 1 / (1 - nw.phi) ** 2

// Demeaned path, sample autocovariances, and both estimators for q = 0, ..., 30
nwEst = {
  const {phi, T} = nw
  const y = new Float64Array(T)
  let prev = nwShocks[0] / Math.sqrt(1 - phi ** 2)
  for (let t = 0; t < T; t++) { prev = phi * prev + nwShocks[t + 1]; y[t] = prev }
  const m = d3.mean(y)
  for (let t = 0; t < T; t++) y[t] -= m
  const H = Math.min(nwMaxQ, T - 1)
  const g = d3.range(H + 1).map(h => {
    let s = 0
    for (let t = h; t < T; t++) s += y[t] * y[t - h]
    return s / T
  })
  return d3.range(H + 1).map(q => ({
    q,
    trunc: g[0] + 2 * d3.sum(d3.range(1, q + 1), h => g[h]),
    nw: g[0] + 2 * d3.sum(d3.range(1, q + 1), h => (1 - h / (q + 1)) * g[h])
  }))
}

// Rule of thumb from Andrews (1991) with rho = 0.25
nwRule = Math.round(0.75 * Math.cbrt(nw.T))
nwAtRule = nwEst[Math.min(nwRule, nwEst.length - 1)].nw

nwDomain = {
  const vals = [nwOmega2, 0, ...nwEst.flatMap(d => [d.trunc, d.nw])]
  const lo = d3.min(vals), hi = d3.max(vals)
  return [lo - 0.06 * (hi - lo), hi + 0.06 * (hi - lo)]
}

Choosing the Bandwidth \(q\)

  • \(q\) trades bias against variance:
    • Small \(q\): ignores the tail autocovariances, so \(\hat{\symbf{\Omega}}_{NW}\) is biased
    • Large \(q\): adds noisy \(\hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h\)’s, and the variance grows like \(q/T\)
  • The MSE-optimal rate is \(q \propto T^{1/3}\) (Andrews (1991))
  • Andrews’ rule for Bartlett weights, with \(\rho\) the first autocorrelation of \(g_t\):

\[ q = \left(\frac{6\rho^2}{(1 - \rho^2)^2}\right)^{1/3} T^{1/3} \]

  • Plug in an estimate of \(\rho\), or a default: \(\rho = 0.25\) gives \(q \approx 0.75\,T^{1/3}\)
  • Example: \(T = 200\) quarters gives \(q \approx 4\)

In Practice

  • If your main results swing with \(q\), that is a bad sign
  • Report \(q\) and keep it fixed throughout the paper
  • Packages use different defaults: check yours and say which one you used
  • In Python: sm.OLS(y, X).fit(cov_type="HAC", cov_kwds={"maxlags": q})
  • Rule of thumb: \(q\) should be “much smaller” than \(T\)
  • Scores are an MDS under your null? Use White, not Newey-West
  • Example: testing for no serial correlation (Hansen, Sec. 14.36)
  • Kernel choice matters much less than the choice of \(q\) (Andrews (1991) compares several)

Takeaways

What to Remember

  • Asymptotic variance of \(\sqrt{T}(\bar y_T - \mu)\): the long-run variance \(\sum_h\gamma_h\), not \(\gamma_0\)
  • MDS scores: White’s estimator. Serially correlated scores: Newey-West
  • Newey-West is positive semi-definite by construction. Choose \(q \propto T^{1/3}\), and report it
  • Coming up in GMM: the efficient weighting matrix is the inverse of a long-run variance
  • We will estimate it exactly as today, with \(\symbf{g}_t\) the moment functions

Appendix

Proof: The Long-Run Variance Limit

  • In proper EPGE style, let \(\varepsilon > 0\), and pick \(q\) such that \(\sum_{h=q+1}^{\infty}|\gamma_h| < \varepsilon/4\)
  • For \(T > q\), subtract the boxed formula from \(\omega^2 = \sum_h\gamma_h\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \left|\omega^2 - T\cdot\E\left(\bar y_T - \mu\right)^2\right| &= \Bigg|\sum_{|h| < T}\frac{|h|}{T}\gamma_h + \sum_{|h| \ge T}\gamma_h\Bigg| && \\ &\le \sum_{h=1}^{q}\frac{2h}{T}|\gamma_h| + \sum_{h=q+1}^{\infty}2|\gamma_h| && (|\text{coefficients}| \le 1)\\ &< \frac{2}{T}\sum_{h=1}^{q}h|\gamma_h| + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} && \text{(choice of } q) \end{aligned} \]

  • With \(q\) fixed, the first term is below \(\varepsilon/2\) for \(T\) large enough. Hence \(T\cdot\E\left(\bar y_T - \mu\right)^2 \to \omega^2\)

Back to the long-run variance

Proof: Newey-West Is Positive Semi-Definite

  • Set \(\hat{\symbf{g}}_s = 0\) for \(s \notin \{1, \ldots, T\}\), and add up blocks of \(q+1\) periods: \(\symbf{B}_t \equiv \sum_{s=t}^{t+q}\hat{\symbf{g}}_s\)

\[ \begin{aligned} \sum_{t}\symbf{B}_t\symbf{B}_t' &= \sum_{s}\sum_{s'}\left(q + 1 - |s - s'|\right)_+\hat{\symbf{g}}_s\hat{\symbf{g}}_{s'}' && (s \text{ and } s' \text{ share this many blocks})\\ &= \sum_{h=-q}^{q}\left(q + 1 - |h|\right)\sum_{s}\hat{\symbf{g}}_s\hat{\symbf{g}}_{s-h}' && (h = s - s')\\ &= T\sum_{h=-q}^{q}\left(q + 1 - |h|\right)\hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h && \text{(definition of } \hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h) \end{aligned} \]

\[ \implies \hat{\symbf{\Omega}}_{NW} = \sum_{h=-q}^{q}\left(1 - \frac{|h|}{q+1}\right)\hat{\symbf{\Gamma}}_h = \frac{1}{T(q+1)}\sum_{t}\symbf{B}_t\symbf{B}_t' \succeq 0 \]

  • Sums of outer products are PSD. Same weights as in \(T\cdot\Var(\bar y_T)\), with \(q+1\) in place of \(T\)
